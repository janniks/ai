DPO's observation is that the RLHF objective has a closed-form optimal policy, and inverting it expresses the reward in terms of the policy itself: $r(x, y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y \mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y \mid x)}$ up to a constant. Substituting into the preference loss gives a single supervised objective,

$$\mathcal{L} = -\mathbb{E}\left[\log \sigma\!\left(\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w \mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_w \mid x)} - \beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l \mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_l \mid x)}\right)\right],$$

trained directly on comparison pairs. No reward model, no sampling loop, no PPO. Constitutional AI attacks the other bottleneck, the human labels: a model critiques and revises its own outputs against written principles, and preference data is generated by AI feedback. The two ideas are orthogonal, and modern pipelines mix both freely.
