DPO's observation is that the RLHF objective has a known best answer in closed form, and inverting it lets the reward be written in terms of the model itself: how much more probable the model in training makes a response, compared to the frozen reference model it started from. Substituting that into the preference loss gives a single supervised objective. Writing $\pi$ for the model being trained, $\pi_\text{ref}$ for the reference, $y_w$ and $y_l$ for the preferred and rejected responses to a prompt $x$, the loss rewards

$$\beta \log \tfrac{\pi(y_w \mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_w \mid x)} - \beta \log \tfrac{\pi(y_l \mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_l \mid x)}$${tip:raise the probability of the preferred answer relative to the reference model, and lower the rejected one, with the number beta controlling how strongly}

being large, trained directly on comparison pairs. No reward model, no sampling loop, no PPO. Constitutional AI attacks the other bottleneck, the human labels: a model critiques and revises its own outputs against written principles, and preference data is generated by AI feedback. The two ideas are independent, and modern pipelines mix both freely.
