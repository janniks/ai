In $d$ dimensions only $d$ vectors can be exactly orthogonal, but exponentially many can be almost orthogonal, and the toy-models paper shows networks exploit this: when features are sparse, rarely active together, a layer can pack many more of them than it has neurons, accepting small interference in exchange for capacity. The result is polysemanticity, single neurons that fire for unrelated concepts, because concepts live in directions, not units.

The countermeasure is dictionary learning. A sparse autoencoder is trained to rewrite activations as sparse combinations of an overcomplete basis, $x \approx \sum_i a_i f_i$ with few $a_i$ nonzero, and the recovered $f_i$ are strikingly more interpretable than neurons. This technique produced the millions of features mapped in Claude.
