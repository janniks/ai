After nine chapters you can build, train, and deploy these models. This chapter asks what they actually learned. The weights are not code anyone wrote; they are the residue of an optimization, and reading them is reverse engineering, closer to biology than to software.

The field's central claims are that networks contain features, directions in activation space that respond to meaningful properties, and circuits, subnetworks of weights connecting features into algorithms. Some have been found and named: curve detectors in vision models, induction heads that implement copying in transformers.

The central obstacle is superposition: networks represent more features than they have dimensions by overlapping them, so individual neurons are rarely individually meaningful. The chapter runs from the program's founding arguments to the open problems where a newcomer can still contribute.
