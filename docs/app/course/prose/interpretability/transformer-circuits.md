The mathematical framework reads an attention head as two separable circuits: the QK circuit, $W_Q^\top W_K$, decides where to attend, and the OV circuit, $W_O W_V$, decides what is moved when attention lands. Because everything is written into a shared residual stream, heads in later layers can read what earlier heads wrote, and compositions of heads implement algorithms.

The induction head is the proof of concept: a two-head circuit that finds the previous occurrence of the current token and copies what followed it, completing the pattern $[A][B] \dots [A] \to [B]$. These heads appear abruptly during training, coincide with a visible bump in the loss curve, and account for a substantial part of in-context learning, a mechanistic explanation of a capability that otherwise looks emergent.
