Kaplan et al. found that test loss falls as a power law, approximately $L(N) \propto N^{-\alpha}$ in parameters and similarly in data and compute, smoothly over many orders of magnitude. Chinchilla corrected the allocation: at a fixed compute budget $C \approx 6ND$, loss is minimized with parameters and tokens scaled together, roughly $D \approx 20N$. GPT-3, trained on far fewer tokens than that, was substantially undertrained for its size.

Two readings of these laws coexist. They are an engineering tool, letting labs predict a frontier model's loss from cheap small-scale runs before committing the budget. And they are a scientific puzzle: nothing in the architecture explains why the exponents are what they are, or why capabilities like in-context learning surface at particular scales along the curve.
