A derivative measures how much a quantity changes when one of its inputs is nudged. Collect all of a model's adjustable numbers into one symbol $\theta$ (theta), and write $\mathcal{L}$ for the loss, the single number measuring how wrong the model is. The gradient, written $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}${tip:read as the gradient of the loss with respect to the parameters: the list of all those nudge-sensitivities at once}, gathers the derivative of the loss with respect to every parameter, and points in the direction that increases the loss fastest. Training is the repeated step $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \, \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}${tip:replace the parameters by themselves minus a small multiple of the gradient, taking a small step downhill}, where $\eta$ (eta) is a small number called the learning rate.

The chain rule is the entire mechanism: when functions are applied one after another, the sensitivity of the final output to the first input is the product of each step's own sensitivity. Backpropagation, the subject of chapter one, is nothing more than evaluating this product efficiently, from the loss backward.
