A vector is a list of numbers, a point or an arrow in space; a matrix is a rectangular grid of numbers. The single operation to internalize is multiplying a matrix by a vector: the matrix $W$ takes in a vector and puts out a new one, and the only moves it can make are rotations, stretches, shears, and flattenings. Every layer of every model in this curriculum is such a map followed by a simple bending operation.

Two consequences matter constantly. The dot product, written $u^\top v${tip:multiply the two lists entry by entry and add the results up; it is large when the arrows point the same way and zero when they are perpendicular}, measures how aligned two vectors are, which is how attention scores and embedding similarity are computed. And applying one matrix after another is itself a single matrix, which is why deep networks are stacks of matrix multiplications and why GPUs, built to multiply matrices, run the whole field.
