Learning is framed as probability. A model with adjustable parameters $\theta$ assigns each possible output $y$ a probability given the input $x$, written $p_\theta(y \mid x)${tip:the probability the model gives to y when shown x; the vertical bar reads as given}. Training adjusts $\theta$ to make the actually observed data as probable as possible; equivalently, it minimizes the sum, over examples, of $-\log p${tip:minus the logarithm of the probability: a penalty near zero when the model was confident and right, and huge when it called the truth nearly impossible}. Nearly every loss in this curriculum is this quantity under another name.

Keep three ideas close: conditional probability, how likely something is once you know something else; expectation, the average outcome weighted by probability; and cross-entropy, which scores one probability distribution by how surprised it is, on average, by samples from another. That last one is the loss function of language modeling.
