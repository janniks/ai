Training minimizes a loss averaged over data, $\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \ell(f_\theta(x_i), y_i)$, by the update $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \, \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$. In practice the gradient is estimated on a small random minibatch rather than the full dataset; the noise this introduces is tolerated, and arguably helpful, in exchange for many more updates per unit of compute.

Momentum smooths the trajectory by accumulating a velocity, $v \leftarrow \beta v + \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$, $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta v$, damping oscillation across narrow valleys. Adam, the default optimizer for the models ahead, additionally rescales each coordinate by a running estimate of its gradient's magnitude, so all parameters move at comparable speeds.
