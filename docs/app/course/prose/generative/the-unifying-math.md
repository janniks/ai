Predicting the noise is equivalent, up to rescaling, to estimating the score: at every point, the direction in which data becomes more typical. Seen this way, sampling is following a learned field of arrows from pure randomness to the data, and the step-by-step denoising chain becomes a continuous flow with a fully deterministic counterpart that permits fast samplers.

Flow matching makes the transport view primary: choose any smooth path between noise and data, often the straight line $x_t = (1-t)\,x_0 + t\,x_1${tip:a blend that starts as pure noise x-zero when the time t is zero and slides linearly to the data point x-one when t reaches one}, and train a network to predict the velocity, the direction and speed of travel along that path. Diffusion falls out as one choice of path. The unification matters practically; the field's current samplers, distillation methods, and video models are mostly statements about which path and which solver.
