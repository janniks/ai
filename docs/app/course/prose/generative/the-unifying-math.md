Predicting the noise is equivalent, up to scaling, to estimating the score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$, the direction in which the noised data density increases. Seen this way, sampling is following a learned vector field from the Gaussian prior to the data distribution, and the discrete denoising chain becomes a stochastic differential equation with a deterministic (ODE) counterpart that permits fast samplers.

Flow matching makes the transport view primary: choose any smooth path between noise and data, often a straight line $x_t = (1-t)\,x_0 + t\,x_1$, and regress a network onto the path's velocity field. Diffusion falls out as one choice of path. The unification matters practically; the field's current samplers, distillation methods, and video models are mostly statements about which path and which solver.
