The forward process adds random noise to an image in many small steps on a fixed schedule, and the steps compound neatly: writing $x_0${tip:read as x-zero, the original picture before any noise has been added. The little zero underneath just labels the starting point of the process, step number zero.} for the clean image, $x_t${tip:read as x-t, the picture after t rounds of noising. The small t underneath is a counter saying how many noise steps have been applied, so bigger t means a fuzzier image.} for its state after $t$ steps, and $\epsilon$ (epsilon) for a fresh draw of pure noise, any example can be noised to any level in one shot,

$$x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\, \epsilon$${tip:read it as: the noisy image at step t equals the square root of alpha-bar-t times the clean image, plus the square root of one minus alpha-bar-t times pure random noise. Alpha-bar-t is just a number from the fixed schedule that slides from one down toward zero as the step count t grows, so early on the mix is almost all image and late in the process it is almost all noise. The point is that you can jump straight to any noise level in a single calculation instead of adding noise step by step.}

The network is shown $x_t${tip:read as x-t, the noised version of the image at step t. This is the only picture the network ever sees during training, never the clean original.} and the step number $t$ and is trained to guess the noise $\epsilon$ that was added, penalized by the squared difference between its guess and the truth, a plain regression. Sampling runs the chain backward, subtracting predicted noise step by step from a start of pure noise. Stable Diffusion adds one economy: the process runs on a small compressed version of the image, produced by a pretrained autoencoder, which is what makes it cheap enough to be everywhere.
