CLIP trains an image encoder and a text encoder so that matching pairs have high cosine similarity and mismatched pairs low, a contrastive objective over millions of captioned images. The result is a shared embedding space where a sentence and the images it describes are neighbors, and this is what lets a prompt steer a denoiser: the text embedding is fed into the UNet through cross-attention at every step.

Classifier-free guidance sharpens the conditioning. The model is evaluated with and without the prompt, and the difference is amplified: $\hat\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t) + w\,(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, c=\varnothing))$ with guidance scale $w > 1$. Higher $w$ buys prompt fidelity at the cost of diversity, the trade you adjust in every image generator's settings.
