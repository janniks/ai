All decoding strategies are different ways of drawing from the same predicted probabilities. Temperature is a number $T$ that divides the model's raw scores before they are turned into probabilities: as $T$ shrinks toward zero the model always picks its single favorite token, and $T$ above one flattens the choices toward uniform. Top-k sampling draws only from the $k$ most probable tokens; top-p (nucleus) sampling keeps the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities add up to at least the chosen $p$, so the cutoff adapts to how confident the model is.

Always taking the favorite, called greedy decoding, is repetitive because probability and quality diverge: the most probable continuation of text is often degenerate. Sampling from a trimmed distribution keeps the long tail of nonsense out while preserving genuine variation. These are inference-time choices; the same model, at different settings, gives a different writer.
